When Do Labradors Stop Pulling on the Leash? What Owners Report by Age

Owner reports, what the research says about timing, and a planner for your own dog.

Home›Blog›When Do Labradors Stop Pulling on the Leash?

When do Labradors stop pulling on the leash? There is no age at which it switches off. Owners in a long Labrador forum thread describe pulling that began around 4.5 months and was still a daily fight at 14 months, and the one who reported a calm dog at 16 months had trained for months to get there.

Quick answer: maturity can take the edge off, but the habit only fades when pulling stops paying off. VCA Animal Hospitals says most dogs need several months of regular practice to learn loose-leash walking. Use the planner below to see what that looks like for your dog.

Two patterns repeat. First, nobody describes pulling vanishing at a birthday. Second, the owner who reported success had worked on it for months. One reply in the thread put it bluntly: this is not something dogs grow out of, you need to train your dog not to do it.

If your Lab is calmer in general as they age, that can help. Our look at when Labradors calm down covers that side, but a calmer dog still needs a trained walk.

Why does pulling often get worse around adolescence?

Several owners place the trouble in the teenage months. One said the "campaign of pulling and lunging" started at about 4.5 months. The same owner said they were going to "persevere through this teenager stage". That is how owners describe it, not a measured finding.

Pulling is also common, not a sign you failed. An owner survey from the University of Edinburgh covering the UK and Ireland (2,092 dogs) found that 82.7% of dogs pulled on the lead. About a third of the pulling dogs, 32%, were walked 30 minutes or less a day, which cuts exercise.

That matters for a Lab. A dog that goes out less builds more energy, and a Lab with extra energy pulls harder. If you are unsure how much exercise is right at each age, see our guide to the better choices than chasing exhaustion.

One note on gear. A 2021 study of shelter dogs found dogs pulled with greater force and for longer in a back-connection harness than a neck collar when lured with food. So a harness alone will not fix pulling. VCA also warns against collars that rely on pain, such as slip, choke, prong or electronic collars.

Which approach fits which Lab? Best-for picks

  • Young puppy: treat luring plus stopping when tight, in short sessions.
  • Teen Lab that surges: direction changes, and a calm "reset" before the pull starts.
  • Lab that is great at home only: quiet-place practice, moving outward one setting at a time.
  • Strong adult with a smaller handler: a well-introduced head collar with a backup leash, plus training.

Walking more than one dog? Our guide on how to walk two Labradors adds leash handling to this list. A dog who is obsessed with fetch may also need a calm-down routine, which we cover in fetch rules and an off switch.

What does the plan look like month by month?

How long should you expect this to take? VCA says several months. Our owners' stories fit that. A simple pattern many of them describe:

  1. Weeks 1 to 4: indoors or in the driveway, rewarding every few steps at your side.
  2. Months 2 to 3: quiet street, stop-and-wait whenever the leash tightens.
  3. Months 3 to 6: add distractions, a little at a time. Expect lapses.

This outline is our synthesis of the owner reports and VCA, not a studied schedule. Your dog may be faster or slower, and size counts. The 25 kg dog in the thread found lunging "painful" for its owner.

Final verdict: what to do now

Do not wait for your Lab to grow out of it. Start with stop-when-tight, add one more approach from the table, and use the planner to track your progress. Questions owners ask about leash pulling

Do Labradors grow out of pulling on the leash?

Not on their own. In a Labrador forum thread an experienced member told a 14-month-old Lab's owner this is not something dogs grow out of and that it has to be trained. Maturity may help, but practice is what changes the habit.

At what age do Labradors start pulling?

It varies. One owner in a Labrador forum thread said their puppy walked nicely at heel until about 4.5 months, then began pulling and lunging. Another owner's dog pulled at 14 months.

How long does loose-leash training take?

VCA Animal Hospitals says it takes most dogs several months of regular practice to learn to walk on a loose leash.

Does a harness stop a Labrador pulling?

Not by itself. A 2021 study of shelter dogs found they pulled with greater force and for longer in a back-connection harness than in a neck collar, so equipment is a tool, not a fix.

Why is my Lab fine at home but pulls outside?

Distractions change the picture. Forum owners describe good walking indoors that falls apart with smells, people or dogs, which is why training is built up one setting at a time.

Is pulling a common problem?

Yes. A University of Edinburgh survey of UK and Ireland owners found that 82.7% of dogs pulled on the lead.

Quick takeaways

Pulling is trained away, not outgrown. Plan on several months of regular practice, and build up from quiet places to busy ones.
