Labrador coat color calculator

Pick the two genes that decide Labrador color for each parent and see the odds of black, chocolate and yellow puppies. It is the same B/E genetics a DNA test reports, in plain English.

Labrador color is decided by two genes working in order. The B locus says whether pigment is black or chocolate. The E locus says whether that pigment can show up in the coat at all. Change either one and the litter changes.

    How the B and E genes work

    B locus (TYRP1 gene). B produces black pigment, b produces chocolate. A dog with at least one B is black-based; a dog with bb is chocolate-based. Chocolate is recessive, so both parents must pass b for a chocolate puppy.

    E locus (MC1R gene). E lets black or chocolate pigment show in the coat. Two copies of e (ee) switch that pigment off in the hair, so the dog looks yellow or golden — while the nose and eye rims keep the colour their B genes set. A yellow Lab with bb has a brown nose; a yellow with B has a black nose.

    That gives three coat colours from two genes. The calculator crosses the possible egg and sperm combinations and adds up the phenotype each genotype produces.

    Reading the results

    The percentages are per puppy, not per litter. A 25% result means each puppy has a one-in-four chance — a litter of eight can still have five chocolates. Real litters also drift from the expected odds simply because each puppy is an independent draw.

    Sanity check: two yellow parents cannot produce black or chocolate puppies, and two chocolate parents cannot produce black. If a litter contradicts that, a parent's genotype is not what it was believed to be.

    Questions owners ask

    Can two yellow Labradors have black puppies?

    No. Yellow means ee, so both parents pass e and every puppy is ee — yellow. The nose colour can still differ depending on B.

    Can two black Labradors have yellow puppies?

    Yes, if both are Ee. Two carriers have a 25% chance per puppy of an ee puppy. If both are also Bb, the same litter can include chocolate too.

    Can two chocolate Labradors have black puppies?

    No. Chocolate is bb, so every puppy gets b from both sides and is bb. Puppies are chocolate, or yellow when ee.

    Why does a yellow Labrador have a brown nose?

    A yellow Lab that is also bb has brown pigment in the nose and eye rims. With at least one B the same yellow has black pigment. A yellow with no pigment is usually called a Dudley.

    Is colour linked to health or temperament?

    No. Coat colour is pigment genetics. Health clearances and temperament come from other genes, breeding choices and training — not from B or E.
